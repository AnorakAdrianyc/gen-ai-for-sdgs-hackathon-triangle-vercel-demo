How a building repair moves, in plain words

Risk map Stakeholder network Repair workflow

Think of it like booking a ride. Someone asks, the app checks who they are, a licensed person is matched, and everyone shares one receipt instead of a private chat. The example below follows sample case NT-441 at North Terrace Sample (fictional).

Starts here

A resident notices a problem

Smoke-stop doors on 12/F do not close.

1

Report

Resident

They file it on their own building — like requesting a trip.

Ride: rider requests a car

2

Check

Platform

Does this account belong to one flat in this building?

No — stop. The case does not leave the flat.

Yes — continue. Identity matches the sample flat on 12/F.

Ride: the app confirms the rider

3

Route

Owners' corporation

The case goes to the building's owners, not a group chat. Case NT-441 lands on the verified OC.

Ride: the trip is offered to the right account

4

Pack

Property manager

The manager writes the job and when someone can come in — fire-door tender pack and an access window.

Ride: dispatcher sets the pickup

5

Match

Contractor

Licensed contractors are offered the job. Each sees the work, not the other person's price.

Ride: a licensed driver is offered the trip

6

Award

Owners' corporation

The owners choose the contractor. The manager alone cannot award the job.

Ride: the account holder confirms the ride

7

Work

Contractor

The contractor does the repair and uploads completion photos onto the same case thread.

8

Sign-off

Licensed inspector

A checker who is not the contractor confirms the doors self-close.

Not good enough — back to work.

Passed — move to update.

Ride: vehicle inspection after the trip

9

Update

Resident & authority desk

The resident sees the case closed and the building's risk colour on the map. The authority desk sees the official trail. Prices stay hidden from residents.

Ride: both sides see the trip receipt
